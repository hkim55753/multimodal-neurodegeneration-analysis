# Alcohol-Related Neurodegeneration and Parkinsonian Syndromes

## Overview

This project investigates neuropsychiatric and neuroimaging correlates of alcohol-related neurodegeneration and Parkinsonian syndromes, with emphasis on cerebellar dysfunction, cognitive impairment, mood symptoms, and overlapping movement disorder phenotypes.

Alcohol-related neurological disease and Parkinsonian syndromes may demonstrate overlapping clinical manifestations including gait instability, executive dysfunction, affective symptoms, and neurocognitive decline. This project explores computational and imaging-informed approaches for characterizing these overlapping neurodegenerative and neuropsychiatric processes.

## Research Objectives

* Investigate neuroimaging correlates of alcohol-related cerebellar degeneration
* Explore overlapping neuropsychiatric features between alcohol-related neurodegeneration and Parkinsonian syndromes
* Develop computational approaches for imaging and clinical phenotyping
* Examine relationships between cerebellar pathology, cognition, mood symptoms, and motor dysfunction
* Explore scalable methods for neuropsychiatric risk stratification in neurodegenerative disease

## Proposed Methods

Planned analyses include:

* Structural MRI analysis
* Cerebellar volumetric assessment
* Clinical-imaging correlation analysis
* Computational phenotyping
* Exploratory clustering of neuropsychiatric symptom profiles
* Regression modeling of cognitive and psychiatric outcomes
* Multimodal integration of imaging and clinical variables

## Data Sources

This project is designed to utilize publicly available neuroimaging and clinical datasets from open-access repositories and neurodegenerative disease research initiatives where applicable.

## Clinical Significance

Alcohol-related neurodegeneration and Parkinsonian disorders contribute substantially to neurological disability, cognitive impairment, psychiatric morbidity, and healthcare burden. Improved computational characterization of overlapping neuropsychiatric and neuroimaging features may support earlier recognition and improved diagnostic stratification of complex neurodegenerative syndromes.

## Status

Early-stage computational neuropsychiatric research initiative in development as part of an ongoing physician-scientist research trajectory in neurodegenerative and psychiatric disorders.
